Method

Five steps for turning felt, qualitative information into decisions people can act on.

The La Matriz method is a repeatable cycle for collecting lived experience, sorting it without flattening it, publishing what it shows, checking it against human limits, and feeding it back in.

Why naming comes first

Most measurement systems name people by their position on a scale of productive output. This method starts from a different premise: how something is named decides whether the person or community being described keeps agency over the description. Color is used as the naming layer because research shows color associations for abstract words are shared and structured, not arbitrary (Guilbeault et al. 2020, p. 1).

We lose touch with ourselves because of the for profit designs of many social interactions. This phenomenon developed from changes in spiritual philosophy informed by science. We started to view the body and the self as something to be dominated. We return to ourselves when we deviate with small changes that allow us to belong socially on our terms.

The five steps at a glance

StepWhat happensColor sequenceCivic-tech & media field
1 · Plant Collect a fragment of lived experience in the person's own words. nude → grey → blue Participatory research, community intake
2 · Harvest Sort fragments into color relations and trajectories. blue → black → yellow → gold Data classification, open standards
3 · Iterate Find what the sort shows for a specific audience. gold → pink → orange → purple Plain-language publishing, participatory policy drafting
4 · Rest & Feel Stop and check the work against consent, rest, and felt response. purple → red → white Consent, rest, harm review
5 · Re-seed Turn what was found into new fragments and begin again. white → green → brown → nude Open source, iterative delivery

Each step begins on the color the previous step ended on. Step 5 ends on nude, where Step 1 begins, so the method is a cycle rather than a one-way path.

Step 1 · Plant

Gather first-person accounts before any categories are applied, so the record starts from how people describe their own situation, including what they feel physically. In a consultation, this is where you begin with the Pluriverse Journal prompts.

nude → grey → blue  ·  approach → cover → collect

nude · approach / jumpName the conditions as fluid energies, not anything specifically as a face. For example: "I'm stressed because I have to write an academic paper for a conference with limited specifications," has context but the opening is rigid so it stays static. "I'm heavy and pressing down on my thoughts," gives back agency and physical context, so it has more room for a dynamic story.

grey · cover / loseDon't aim for precision. Aim for accuracy. Instead of trying to name the content, try to capture the qualities in the name. Metaphors and symbols may be helpful.

blue · collect / gleanName with the complementary binaries in mind, a paradox true on both sides. Your blue notebook: you love it for holding insights, and sometimes hate it for fear of having nothing to add or running out of pages. Intellect mixed with sadness. The tension itself is the data you collect.

Step 2 · Harvest

Sort the collected fragments into relations, not rankings. Each fragment is mapped onto La Matrice's 15 colors and traced as a trajectory, so the structure is visible without reducing a person to one score. In a consultation, this is the Color Plotter: your words mapped to named sequences in a custom PDF.

blue → black → yellow → gold  ·  collect → order → structure → pull

blue · collect / gleanYour fragments may sound encoded, and that will bring more richness in context to the analysis.

black · order / scaleThe names of the fragments only mark positions that build into trajectories, allowing you to choose your path.

yellow · structure / arrayWords cluster according to matched color distribution with statistical significance.

gold · pull / hideColors are structured so they can preserve binaries, without collapsing into them. They allow for nuanced categorization.

Step 3 · Iterate

Find what the sort shows for a specific audience, in language that audience already uses. Each audience gets a deliberate level of openness: resonant enough to be recognized, protected enough that the work is not taken out of context.

gold → pink → orange → purple  ·  pull → category → hit → free

gold · pull / hideChoosing the audience you write for helps you preserve your integrity in the relation.

pink · category / placeNaming is the catalyst between being measured and being embraced. Don't just mark a position, but capture change to keep the relationality intact.

orange · hit / dropProtect the right to exclude people from a conversation in order to preserve the ones you connect with. Rather than closing people off, this opens the concept for relation.

purple · free / cheerIf you embrace your community through your naming, you preserve the contexts involved from appropriation.

Step 4 · Rest & Feel

Stop before the next cycle and check the work against human limits: consent, rest, and how it actually felt to receive. Decisions are not made mid-reaction; they are made once the response has settled.

purple → red → white  ·  free → limit → hope

purple · free / cheerA decision is a rotation, not a point. We are amidst binaries, but the states between choices are more complex.

red · limit / cryNaming a limitation is information, not failure, and allows for another rotation.

white · hope / blockWe can reclaim the self when we make changes that preserve place, but not relation.

Step 5 · Re-seed

Turn what was found and felt into new fragments, and start the cycle again. Tools and data are shared openly so others can run the same cycle in their own communities.

white → green → brown → nude  ·  hope → transition → learn → approach

white · hope / blockTo embrace yourself, a relationship to feeling exterior is required.

green · transition / growWe need maintenance and repair for the concepts that remain fragile and uncertain.

brown · learn / shrinkRather than separating life and agency from objects, this method aims for a unified ontology.

nude · approach / jumpHow can you be more active in your claiming and calling when you repeat the cycle?
